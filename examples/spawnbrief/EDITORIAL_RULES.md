# SpawnBrief — standing editorial rules

Updated from the channel owner's feedback, 2026-09-18. Apply to future
SpawnBrief shorts and revisions. These rules supersede older calendar examples,
multi-game formats and guidance favoring ultra-short edits or rapid cuts. The
18 September header, caption and subscription rules below supersede older
inconsistent title treatments, low captions and the single-CTA-only convention.

## Priority: show the gameplay clearly

- Every video covers exactly **one game**. A promotion video covers exactly
  **one offer for that game**. No multi-game lists or promotion roundups.
- Well-presented, varied gameplay is the highest creative priority. Give viewers
  enough time to see what happens and get acquainted with the game or offer.
- After the roughly three-second opening, include **at least 20 seconds of
  substantive gameplay with narration about the game**, before the closing CTA.
  The opening, subscription reminder and closing CTA do not count toward that
  minimum. Longer is appropriate when the explanation or action needs it;
  shortest runtime is not the goal.
- Show several distinct, strong scenes demonstrating different actions,
  mechanics or situations. Hold each shot long enough to understand the action.
  Cut around the gameplay and explanation, not a fixed 0.8–1.5-second interval.
- Do not replay the same source moment within a video. A crop, zoom, reversal
  or speed change does not turn reused footage into a new scene. Check source
  intervals and actual visuals for repetition. Simultaneous background and
  foreground copies of one shot are one composition, not two distinct scenes.
- For later videos about the same game, choose fresh moments instead of
  recycling the same few scenes. If there are too few useful scenes, find more
  appropriate footage within the authorized scope. Do not fill the duration
  with loops, static filler, slowed speech or extra silence.

## Game selection: Steam reviews

- Before selecting a game, check the current **All Reviews** summary on its
  Steam store page. The minimum eligible label is **Positive**.
- Accept only **Positive**, **Very Positive** or **Overwhelmingly Positive**.
  Exclude **Mostly Positive**, **Mixed** and every lower rating. A large review
  count does not override the label; for example, `Mixed (1,778)` is ineligible.
- A game without a qualifying Steam **All Reviews** label is outside the normal
  SpawnBrief scope. Recheck the rating during research because it can change.

## Opening: a natural introduction over gameplay

- The first roughly three seconds are part of the video, with relevant gameplay
  already visible behind the opening sentence. No separate hook card, teaser
  montage, logo splash, menu footage or title-only intro.
- Useful English patterns: “In this game, you and your friends…”, “In this
  puzzle-filled game, you…”, “If you like something weird, this game is for
  you…”, or “In this game, you play as…”. Immediately connect the opening to
  a real mechanic, role or experience shown on screen. Mention friends only
  when the actual game mode supports it.
- Owner examples: “W tej grze wraz z kolegami…”, “W tej grze pełnej zagadek…”,
  “Jeśli lubisz coś dziwnego, to ta gra jest dla ciebie…”. These express the
  structure; the channel's viewer-facing language remains English.
- Promotion pattern: “Another game you can claim for free on [platform] — this
  time it is [game]…”. Name the game naturally during the introduction.
- Verify the game, mechanics, platforms and release status with current official
  sources. For an offer, also verify claim conditions and deadline/timezone.
  Distinguish a keep-forever giveaway from a free weekend, demo, subscription
  benefit or discount. Do not invent features or use stale promotion claims.

## Closing: a concrete invitation to respond

- At the end, ask one relevant, easy-to-answer question with a clear comment
  action. This is in addition to the single early subscription reminder below.
- Multiplayer: “Who would you play this with? Tag them in the comments.”
- Experience: “Already played it? Tell us what you think in the comments.”
- Adapt to the game. Do not tack on an unrelated “Who wins?”, extra like/share
  requests or a second subscription reminder. Do not stack requests in one beat.
- Let the spoken question finish; cut shortly afterwards without a dead end card.

## Consistent header and phone-safe narration captions

- Use the **DIMRAETH screenshot's header with the short horizontal accent dash**
  for every film. In Synkinema this is the `editorial` title style: bold white
  uppercase game name, a short colored dash above it, and an optional verified
  status such as “Early Access” below in the accent color. Reuse the same font,
  weights, alignment, spacing and treatment; only the accent color may vary to
  suit the footage. Game name/status content changes with the game. Do not
  substitute the plain outlined WARDOGS/SOLARPUNK heading style.
- At 1080×1920 use header font size 72, `text_x=0.06`, `text_y=0.16` and the
  editorial style's standard dash/subtitle treatment. Wrap a long title to at
  most two lines within the safe width instead of changing the design. Keep the
  actual header foreground between x=6–82% and y=14–31% of the exported frame.
- Narration captions use a consistent compact `boxed` treatment: white bold
  text on a tightly fitted dark translucent background, default font size **44**
  at 1080×1920. Use short speech-aligned phrases, usually 3–6 words, at most two
  lines. Split a long phrase instead of making a wide or three-line block.
- Start narration captions around **y=59%** of the exported frame (about
  **1133 px**); the complete visible caption including box, stroke and padding
  must fit between **x=6–82%** (65–886 px) and **y=56–66%** (1075–1267 px).
  Center the actual caption block around x=44%, left of the right action rail.
  Font size may be adjusted within 40–48 only if phone readability requires it.
  Check measured raster bounds; a text anchor alone does not prove safe layout.
- Treat the bottom 34%, rightmost 18% and top 14% as reserved for the mobile
  player UI for critical editorial copy. These are conservative SpawnBrief
  design margins derived from the owner's screenshots, not a universal YouTube
  specification. Avoid covering gameplay targets/HUD; if necessary move captions
  upward within the safe band or adjust the gameplay framing.
- Review exports at phone size with representative Shorts UI overlaid, including
  channel name, description, labels, lower controls and right action buttons.
  Check the longest caption and two-line cases. Plain-video previews and a
  no-clipping report alone cannot establish that the platform UI leaves text clear.

## One early subscription reminder

- After the natural opening and first meaningful game description, once the
  viewer understands the premise, insert one short spoken subscription reminder
  at a sentence boundary. Usually this is around 5–8 seconds, not at frame zero;
  choose timing from the actual speech, not a forced timestamp.
- English example: **“Don't forget to subscribe for more games like this.”**
  Keep it brief and conversational, about 2.5–3.5 seconds at the established male
  voice's natural pace. Do not speed up or cut off words to meet a duration.
- While this sentence is spoken, show a reusable **transparent PNG graphic with
  red “Subscribe” lettering** near the top, with enough contrast against gameplay.
  Keep its complete bounds inside x=6–82%, y=15–25%. Red is fixed for this cue;
  only the normal game header's accent color follows the film's palette.
- Temporarily hide the regular game header while the Subscribe graphic is
  visible, then restore the identical header. Sync the image to the real spoken
  sentence and keep its subtitles in the narration-caption safe area. Gameplay
  continues throughout; resume the game explanation immediately afterwards.
- Use this reminder once per video, plus the existing one contextual comment
  question at the end. Its duration does not count toward the minimum 20 seconds
  of substantive narrated gameplay. The graphic is an in-video cue, not a claim
  that viewers can click it.

## Voice, captions and timing

- Preserve the video's established language unless the owner requests a change.
  Current English shorts use a consistent male voice with natural, clear,
  conversational delivery. Do not rush speech to shorten the video.
- Plan enough useful explanation and distinct footage for the minimum gameplay
  section before recording. Measure actual leading/trailing silence and remove
  excess gaps while retaining breaths, question pauses and time to understand
  the action. Silence trimming is cleanup, not a mandate to minimize runtime.
  If the script is too short, improve its useful content and source selection.
- Align phrase captions to the new recording; changing script metadata alone
  does not update voice audio or captions.
- Apply the numeric caption/header safe areas above, including to the subscription
  sentence. New subscription speech requires a real take and resynchronized
  captions, visuals, music and ending.
- Do not burn in an “AI-generated” / “AI voice” footer, technical production
  notes or similar microtext. Do not automatically add such marketing copy to
  the upload description. Preserve truthful internal asset provenance/licences;
  any mandatory licence or platform disclosure must be handled separately at
  publication, not concealed or replaced by an unreadable footer.
- Retain the user's chosen music/mix unless a change is needed for this revision.
  Music and every other track must stop at the intended picture end; no black tail.
- Hashtags, when used, are lowercase. No claims that a hook guarantees virality.

## Final check

Confirm a qualifying Steam **All Reviews** label, one game/offer, at least 20
seconds of narrated gameplay excluding the opening and both CTA beats, several
distinct scenes, no repeated source moments and sufficient time to follow each
action. Inspect the
first frame, opening, standard dash header, subscription graphic and speech,
transitions, closing CTA and final frame from the exact export. Check the compact,
raised captions against representative phone UI. Listen to the narration, check
caption timing and decoded audio. Preserve the previous project revision for
Undo. Do not publish/upload without a request.
